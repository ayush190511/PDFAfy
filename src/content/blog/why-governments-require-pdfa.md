Around the globe, federal agencies, judicial courts, and regulated industries are mandating PDF/A for document submission and record management. Understanding these statutory requirements ensures your organization avoids compliance penalties or filing rejections.

---

## Global Regulatory Mandates

### 1. United States Federal Courts (CM/ECF)
The US Federal Court Case Management/Electronic Case Files (CM/ECF) system requires e-filed legal briefs and court exhibits to comply with PDF/A standards. Un-embedded fonts or dynamic links can cause automated system rejections.

### 2. European Union (eIDAS & GDPR Record Keeping)
Under European regulatory frameworks, electronic signatures and digital archives must meet strict long-term validation (LTV) rules. PDF/A guarantees that signed legal documents remain verifiable over 30+ year statutory retention periods.

### 3. Germany & France E-Invoicing Mandates
German **ZUGFeRD** and French **Factur-X** standards legally require B2B e-invoices to be structured in **PDF/A-3** format with an embedded XML data packet for automated tax auditing.

---

## Why Regulators Reject Standard PDFs

- **Risk of Alteration**: Dynamic JavaScript or interactive form scripts can change displayed text depending on current system date or user actions.
- **Font Licensing & Rendering Errors**: Courts cannot risk judicial documents rendering missing text boxes or altered number values.
- **Security Vulnerabilities**: Executable objects embedded in standard PDFs pose cybersecurity threats to state IT infrastructure.
