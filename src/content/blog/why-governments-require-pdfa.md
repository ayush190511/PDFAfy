# Why Governments & Legal Systems Require PDF/A Compliance

Across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific, national archivists, federal court systems, and regulatory authorities have established strict policies requiring electronic submissions to conform to the **ISO 19005 PDF/A standard**.

---

## Global Regulatory Mandates

### 1. United States Federal Courts (CM/ECF)
The U.S. Federal Judiciary electronic case filing system mandates PDF/A compliance for legal filings to prevent document corruption, preserve evidence integrity, and guarantee readability for decades of court proceedings.

### 2. European Union Directives (eIDAS & Electronic Invoicing)
EU regulation enforces PDF/A-3 (specifically ZUGFeRD / Factur-X standards) for cross-border electronic procurement, tax records, and public administration archives.

### 3. National Archives (UK, Germany, France, Australia)
National archives mandate PDF/A format for all digital government deposits, ensuring public records remain readable without relying on commercial software vendors.

---

## Why PDF/A is the Only Acceptable Archival Standard

1. **Evidentiary Integrity**: Courts require proof that a document presented in a trial looks identically formatted to when it was originally filed.
2. **Vendor Independence**: Government records cannot depend on proprietary software updates or specific operating system versions.
3. **Automated Validation**: PDF/A files contain standardized XMP metadata packets (`pdfaid:part`), allowing government archival intake tools to validate compliance programmatically.
